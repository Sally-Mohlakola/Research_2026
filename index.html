I asked whether a network can learn how light moves inside a diamond. It learned, and became more accurate. The diamonds it rendered matched the real ones less.

[ the journey ]

Three attempts

↱ Step 1 · radiance mapAug

The average

The cloth model's map records how light leaves for each incoming direction. Grouping by direction predicts the exit 6.8% of the time; grouping by where light enters, 19.2%.

↱ Step 2 · LEAN-inspired spreadSep

The spread

Restoring each bin's spread brings back the right amount of sparkle, at random places. Speckle, not sparkle.

↱ Step 3 · neural transport operatorSep – Oct

The position

A network that knows where light enters and predicts whether it escapes, through which facet, and where. One call replaces the whole interior.

↱ Coverage · aim offset2.68B rays

Where rays are aimed

Aimed at the centre, 2.68 billion rays filled at most 41 of 64 bins. Aimed across a disc, 138,000 filled all 64. The same fix lit the pear's underside: escape 46% → 100%.

Same training data, three ways to place the exit. One hedges across a mixture of four Gaussians. Two commit to a single answer.

Where does it go wrong? On long paths. Every internal facet edge light crosses can send it out of a different facet, and a small, smooth network blends between answers.
