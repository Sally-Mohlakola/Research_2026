Neural Diamond Rendering

Honours research · 2026

Can a network learn sparkle?

A diamond's beauty lives in sharp shards of light that flash and vanish as it turns. I asked whether a small neural network can replace the light transport inside a diamond. Across 45 trained networks and three cuts, making the network more accurate made the diamond look more real, yet match the real one less.

01
The question

Cloth forgives averages. Diamonds don't.

Neural appearance models built for cloth learn how light leaves a material for each incoming direction. Cloth fibres are far smaller than a pixel, so that is enough. A diamond is the opposite: its large facets are the appearance, and where light enters decides where it leaves.

Can a small neural network replace the light transport inside a diamond, and if not, which of its errors explain why the image differs?

H1 · Accuracy is not appearance

The decoder that predicts exits most accurately does not give the image that agrees best with the reference.

H2 · Where errors arise

Errors grow as light meets more facets inside the diamond, and are worst at the pavilion.

H3 · Cost

A network query is faster than tracing, yet a full neural render is neither faster nor as accurate.

02
The journey

Three attempts at the same diamond

Each step fixed what the last one could not, and each failure pointed at the next idea.

Step 1 · Radiance map

The average

A histogram of how light leaves for each incoming direction, after Soh & Montazeri's cloth model. It records only direction: grouping paths by direction predicts the exit triangle 6.8% of the time, by entry triangle 19.2%.

Result: flat, blocky shading
Step 2 · LEAN-inspired spread

The variance

Like LEAN mapping, store each bin's spread as well as its mean, and light each point with probability 1/(1+r²). The right amount of sparkle returns, but the bins don't know where on the diamond it belongs.

Result: speckle, not sparkle
Step 3 · Neural transport operator

The position

A network that knows where light enters the diamond and predicts whether it escapes, through which facet, and in which direction.

Result: right outline and energy, soft sparkle
03
How it works

One network call replaces the inside of the diamond

The surface is traced exactly. Everything that happens inside is replaced by one prediction, trained on about a million paths per cut from an exact spectral path tracer, to convergence, with five training seeds each: 45 runs in total.

Stochastic

Learns a spread of possible exits, a four-Gaussian mixture, and samples one each time. When unsure, it hedges.

Clone

The standard baseline: predicts one exit by behavioural cloning. When unsure, it commits.

Deep decoder

A deeper clone with its own encoder. More capacity, still one committed exit.

05
Where it fails

Short paths survive. Long paths don't.

Light inside a diamond jumps: crossing one internal facet edge sends it out of a different facet entirely. The longer the path, the more edges it crosses, and a small smooth network can only blend between answers.

06
A bug worth finding

The underside went dark

Training rays fired only from the camera never enter through the pavilion: 0% of them. So when the diamond tumbled and showed its underside, the network had never seen that light, and lost half of it. Adding rays from every direction, shifted across the diamond with an aim offset, fixed it.

07
Cost and transfer

Faster per decision, slower per image

One learned decision takes 10.5 µs against 31 µs to trace it. Yet a full neural render takes 345 s against 264 s, because gathering, preparing and decoding the network's batches costs more than the tracing it replaces. At equal render time, the analytic image is 7–10 times more accurate.

Making the network more accurate made the diamond look more real, and match the real one less.

H1 holds

Operator accuracy does not reach the image: the sharper decoders agree less on every cut.

H2 in part

Errors follow path length. The pavilion only fails when it is missing from training.

H3 holds

Each decision is faster, yet renders are 31% slower and never reach analytic quality.
